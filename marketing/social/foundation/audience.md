## Primary segment: the serious collector
> Build for them first. They have the problem worst, they post about shirts already, and their
> posts recruit the next wave.

## Segment 1 — The serious collector ⭐ primary
- **Who (relevant traits only):** Owns 20–300+ shirts. Hunts on Vinted, eBay, Depop, retro shops
  and car boots. Knows seasons, suppliers, sponsors, and player vs fan versions. Mostly men aged
  18–40, but that doesn't change the content, the shirts do. UK, Spain, Italy, Netherlands,
  Germany and the US are strong markets (assumed, from where the retro-shirt trade is loudest).
- **Buyer vs follower:** The same person. Pro's price (€4.99/month) is a personal decision.
- **Jobs-to-be-done:**
  - Functional: know exactly what's in the collection, find a specific shirt, avoid buying a
    duplicate while standing in a shop, see the gaps (a missing season, a missing country).
  - Emotional: the satisfaction of order, pride in the collection, the collection "feeling real".
  - Social: be seen as a collector with taste and knowledge, and get kudos from other
    collectors.
- **Pains:**
  - Existing catalogue apps mean typing every detail or searching a database shirt by shirt
    (competitor apps list fields like size, sleeve, name set, patch, condition, price).
  - Spreadsheets get abandoned after 30 rows.
  - Photos are scattered across the camera roll.
  - Forgetting what you own. "Did I already buy the 2006 away?"
- **Desires:** An archive that's done, not a project. Seeing the whole collection at a glance.
  Something worth screenshotting.
- **Beliefs:** "Cataloguing is a chore I'll do someday." "AI will get my obscure shirts wrong."
- **Objections (content should answer these):**
  - "Will it know a 1990s Bundesliga third shirt?" → Show hard cases, the confidence %, and the
    edit step.
  - "Another app that'll disappear with my data?" → Open source, full account deletion, private
    storage.
  - "25 shirts isn't enough." → True for big collections. Pro is coming, so be honest and point
    to the waiting list.
  - "I have to photograph 200 shirts?" → Bulk add: 20 photos at a time (Pro).
- **Language bank (their words, (assumed) until verified against real comments):**
  - Problem: "my collection's got out of hand", "I've lost track", "do I already have this one?"
  - Desire: "the whole collection in one place", "finally catalogued it"
  - Objection: "I'll never get round to logging them all"
  - They call it: "kit", "shirt", "the collection", "my wardrobe", "the grail" (dream shirt),
    "BNWT" (brand new with tags), "player issue", "match worn". Not "jersey" (UK/EU) and not
    "items".
- **Where they are:** Instagram (collection accounts, #footballshirts #classicfootballshirts
  #vintagefootballshirts, flat-lays, "shirt of the day"). TikTok (hauls, unboxings,
  charity-shop finds, rate-my-collection). Reddit r/footballshirts (IDs, legit checks). X
  (kit launches and leaks).
- **Who/what they follow & engage with:** Retro shirt retailers, kit-leak and kit-news
  accounts, kit designers, other collectors' "shirt of the day" posts. They engage with
  identification debates ("which season is this?"), rankings, and nostalgia.
- **Sophistication:** Expert on kits, casual on tech. Get a kit detail wrong and you lose them.
- **Transformation (before → after):** A wardrobe of half-remembered shirts and a camera roll →
  a searchable vault with every shirt, season and country on a map.

## Segment 2 — The growing collector
- **Who:** 5–30 shirts: their club's last few seasons, holiday buys, a couple of retro pieces.
  Starting to call it "a collection".
- **Jobs:** Get organised early. See progress ("I'm at 18"). Plan the next buy.
- **Pains:** Doesn't know seasons and versions well enough to log them. Feels like a "beginner".
- **Why KitVault fits:** The AI fills in what they don't know (season, version, manufacturer).
  Free's 25 shirts covers them completely.
- **Content that lands:** "How to tell which season your shirt is from", starter-collection
  ideas, the "your first 10 shirts" style.
- **Sophistication:** Beginner to intermediate. Explain terms (player vs fan version).

## Segment 3 — Developers & indie makers (secondary, build-in-public)
- **Who:** Developers following solo builders on X and LinkedIn. Some are football fans too.
- **Buyer vs follower:** Mostly followers and amplifiers, not users. The overlap (developers
  who collect shirts) is small but real.
- **Jobs:** Learn how a real AI product is shipped (Gemini Vision, quotas, rate limits, Next.js
  16, Supabase RLS). Follow an honest build story.
- **What earns their attention:** Real numbers, real problems. Examples: the quota and rate-limit
  design, the 429 handling, server-side map projection, i18n without a library.
- **Rule:** Every build post ends at the product, e.g. "Try it for free if you own more than 3
  shirts". Never pitch KitVault to developers as a developer tool.
